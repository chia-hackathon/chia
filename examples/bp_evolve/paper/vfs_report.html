What VFS Rewards: An Audit of the CBP-NG
Scoring Function under Automated Search

CHIA Branch-Predictor Evolution Study
Automated hardware/software co-design with CHIA
Generated from sweep bp_evolve, generations 0–10
Abstract—The Championship Branch Prediction Next Generation (CBP-NG) contest replaces raw misprediction rate with VFS, a voltage-frequency-scaled speedup that folds prediction throughput, misprediction cost and predictor energy into a single number. We treat VFS not as a ranking device but as an optimization target, and ask what an automated search actually finds when VFS is the only signal it receives. We built a three-tier evaluation cascade on CHIA—CBP-NG for scoring, ChampSim for port fidelity, gem5 for a pipeline-depth sweep—and ran eleven generations of MAP-Elites over the template parameters of the shipped TAGE predictor, scoring 29 designs on all 126 inner traces of the official set. Three results follow. First, over the scored population VFS correlates strongly with prediction throughput (r = +0.890) and with first-level predictor latency (r = −0.833), and not at all with accuracy (r = −0.024 against MPKI spanning 5.08–10.09). Second, the gem5 tier does not discriminate: core IPC at the shipped depth varies by 0.51% across ten promoted designs, and its apparent agreement with Tier-1 MPKI (r = −0.908) is carried by a single outlier and inverts to +0.884 when two points are removed. Third, the ranking VFS produces at the shipped depth of nine is not depth-robust, and the failure is attributable: at every depth beyond twelve, every discordant pair in the elite ranking involves the one archived design with high MPKI. Accuracy governs depth robustness, and accuracy is exactly what the depth-nine score does not measure.
Index Terms—branch prediction, CBP-NG, design-space exploration, quality diversity, MAP-Elites, simulation methodology, agentic co-design.

I. Introduction

A scoring function used to rank a fixed slate of contest entries and a scoring function used to steer an automated search are not the same artifact. The first needs only to order designs a human already thought worth submitting. The second is climbed: whatever it rewards, a search will supply, including rewards its designers did not intend to offer. This paper audits the CBP-NG scoring function in the second role.

A. CBP-NG

The Championship Branch Prediction Next Generation contest distributes a simulator, a set of 168 instruction traces, and a reference predictor written in HARCOM, a restricted C++ subset in which storage is declared as typed RAM arrays with explicit read and write ports, so that a predictor's latency and energy can be derived from its source rather than asserted by its author. Entries are ranked by VFS—Voltage-Frequency-Scaled speedup—which combines three measured quantities: how many predictions the design delivers per cycle, how many cycles it loses to mispredictions at an assumed pipeline depth, and how much dynamic energy it spends per instruction. VFS is a deliberate improvement on ranking by misprediction rate alone, which rewards predictors that cannot be built. Our question is what it rewards instead.

B. ChampSim

ChampSim is a trace-driven out-of-order core simulator widely used in branch and cache prediction research. It models a real reorder buffer, so a mispredicted branch costs what the machine's actual occupancy makes it cost. We use ChampSim as a port-fidelity tier: a design that scores well in CBP-NG must be re-expressed against ChampSim's branch-predictor interface and must reproduce its CBP-NG conditional MPKI within tolerance. This catches the failure mode in which a CBP-NG design scores well because of something the CBP-NG harness permits and no core would.

C. gem5

gem5's O3 model is an execution-driven, cycle-level core in which pipeline depth is a parameter rather than a constant. CBP-NG charges misprediction at a fixed nine-stage penalty. Whether a ranking obtained under that assumption survives a machine that is deeper or shallower is an empirical question, and gem5 is where we ask it, sweeping depth over {3, 6, 9, 12, 16, 20, 25, 30}.

D. CHIA

CHIA is an open-source framework for agentic hardware/software co-design that expresses a workflow as a graph of typed nodes over a Ray-based runtime, with each node type pinned to its own container image and its own pool of cluster resources. It is what makes a three-simulator cascade practical: CBP-NG scoring, ChampSim porting and gem5 depth sweeps are three incompatible toolchains, and CHIA runs them as one flow with independently scaled pools (cbp_ng, champsim, gem5), a single lineage database, and the same graph running on a workstation or across cloud VMs.

II. Related Work

Branch predictor championships. The CBP series has driven predictor research for two decades, and the TAGE family [1], [2] remains the reference against which entries are measured. The metric has evolved from misprediction rate toward cost-aware scoring precisely because rate alone is unphysical; CBP-NG's VFS is the current endpoint of that evolution.

Simulator fidelity. A long line of work establishes that simulator-relative conclusions do not transfer freely: ChampSim, gem5 and custom harnesses disagree in magnitude and sometimes in order [3]–[5]. Cross-simulator validation is recommended practice, but is usually performed once, on a final design, rather than being wired into the search that produced it.

Automated architecture search. Design-space exploration by evolutionary and learned methods is well established, and MAP-Elites and quality-diversity search [6], [7] are attractive here because they retain a front of behaviorally distinct designs rather than collapsing to a single optimum. Recent work applies large language models to hardware design generation and repair; our harness supports an LLM arm, but every number in this paper comes from the offline control arm, which mutates template parameters with no model in the loop.

III. What the Existing Setting Lacks

Three gaps motivate this study.

The metric is never audited as an objective. VFS is validated as a ranking of submitted entries. Nobody has reported what a search does when VFS is the sole gradient—which of its three inputs actually carries the optimizer, and which are decorative. A term that changes the score by a few parts per thousand is not a design pressure, however principled its derivation.

Cross-simulator checks are terminal, not structural. Validation usually happens once at the end. But the useful question is whether the ranking the cheap tier produces survives the expensive one, and that question can only be asked if the expensive tier is inside the loop, scoring the same lineage.

Depth is assumed, not swept. CBP-NG's nine-stage penalty is a fixed constant in the scoring path. A predictor that wins at depth nine may or may not win at depth twenty-five, and no contest result reports which.

IV. What We Achieve

  1. A three-tier evaluation cascade on CHIA in which CBP-NG scoring, ChampSim port verification and a gem5 depth sweep run as one flow over one lineage database, so every design carries all three tiers' verdicts.
  2. A numerical audit of VFS over a search-generated population, identifying which of its inputs the score is actually sensitive to.
  3. A depth-robustness analysis that does not merely report that the depth-nine ranking degrades, but isolates which design makes it degrade and why.
  4. A re-scoring identity that lets the full depth sweep be recomputed from counters measured once, making an eight-depth sweep free relative to one CBP-NG pass.

V. Methodology

A. Nomenclature

Three distinct quantities are called “IPC” in this pipeline, and conflating them invalidates everything downstream. Table I separates them.

T is a throughput, not a core IPC: it is how many predictions the predictor supplies per cycle. C is a penalty, not a period: it is cycles lost to mispredictions per instruction. EPI is normalized so the reference predictor scores 1000.

B. The scoring function

Let ρ = 0.05 be the fraction of system energy attributed to the predictor, Tref = 8, Cref = 0.0315, α = 1.625, γ = 2/(α−1) = 3.2 and β = 4α/(α−1)² = 16.64. Define the reference and actual work-per-instruction

(1)wref = TrefCref = 0.252,  w = TC.

The speedup term is

(2)S = (T / Tref) · (1 + wref) / (1 + w).

With λ = (1 + wref/2)−1 − ρ = 0.838099, the normalized energy is

(3)E = ( (EPI/1000)ρ + λSγ )(1 + w/2),

and the score is

(4)VFS = Sα( 1 − 2 / (1 + √(1 + β/(SE)) ) ).

Equation (2) is the structural heart of the audit. Throughput T enters S linearly and in the numerator. Accuracy enters only through w = TC in the denominator, where C ≪ 1, so a design that doubles its misprediction cost moves 1 + w from 1.05 to 1.10—a few percent—while a design that adds one prediction per cycle moves S proportionally. Energy is weighted by ρ = 0.05 before it is added to a term of order one. The score is therefore a throughput metric with accuracy and energy corrections, and the search finds this out immediately.

C. Aggregation and the re-scoring identity

Per trace, with p1, p2 the first- and second-level predictor latencies in cycles, cycles are accumulated as Np max(1, p2) when p2 ≤ p1, and as Np max(1, p1) + Ndp2 − Ne max(1, p1) otherwise, for Np predictions, Nd divergences and Ne divergences at end of trace; T is the harmonic mean of Ninst/cyc over traces. The misprediction cost is

(5)C = mpi · ( D + p2 − max(1, min(p1, p2)) ),

where D is pipeline depth and mpi is mispredictions per instruction. Equation (5) is the only place D appears. Consequently the entire depth sweep is a closed-form re-scoring of counters measured once: eight depths cost one CBP-NG pass, not eight. We verified our reimplementation reproduces every stored VFS to better than 10−4 and the stored depth sweep exactly.

D. Search

We use MAP-Elites over a behavior grid keyed on (EPI band, p1 latency, p2 latency), nominally 6×4×6 = 144 cells. Parents are drawn uniformly over occupied cells, not proportionally to VFS; this is what keeps behaviorally distinct designs alive and matters in Section VII. The offline arm perturbs exactly two of eight template parameters of the seed TAGE per proposal, clipped to declared ranges. It changes no algorithm: the source diff between the seed and any offline descendant is the struct name.

E. Tiers

Tier 0 scores every proposal on all 126 inner traces. Tier 1 re-expresses the best designs against ChampSim and requires conditional MPKI agreement within tolerance. Tier 2 promotes one design per generation to gem5 and sweeps depth.

VI. Experimental Setup

We ran eleven generations (0–10), three variants per generation, on the official 168-trace CBP-NG set split 126 inner / 42 held out, with every trace run to its end and 106 warm-up instructions. Thirty-one designs were proposed; 29 scored and two failed at the run stage. Eleven designs reached Tier 1 and ten reached Tier 2. Generations 0–2 ran on four t2d-standard-8 cloud VMs plus a local pool; generations 3–10 ran on a single workstation with 16 concurrent CBP-NG slots, resumed from the generation-2 archive. Because MAP-Elites' search state is the archive, and each archived elite stores its own template-parameter vector, the parameter walk continues across that boundary; we verified this directly, and every generation-3 variant is two parameters from its parent rather than from the seed.

All VFS values reported here are in-sample. The 42 held-out traces have not been scored, and no claim in this paper is a generalization claim.

VII. Results

A. Finding 1: VFS tracks throughput, not accuracy

Over the 29 scored designs (Table II), VFS correlates strongly with prediction throughput and with first-level latency, weakly with energy, and not at all with accuracy—despite MPKI spanning a factor of two, from 5.08 to 10.09. This is not a small-sample effect; it is Equation (2) made visible.

The best design found, gen007_2 at VFS = 0.9256, is the seed with two parameters changed: the fetch-line width LOGLB 6 → 7, which doubles delivered predictions per cycle from 16 to 32, and LOGB 12 → 11, which merely rebalances the bimodal index. It is not more accurate than the seed—MPKI rises from 5.338 to 5.462—and it is not larger, at 34.5 KiB against the seed's 35.0 KiB. It is simply wider. Conversely the most accurate design found, gen010_2 at MPKI = 5.084, spends 72.25 KiB, more than twice the seed, and scores VFS = 0.7444: second worst among archived elites.

A single derived parameter explains the accuracy axis. The number of hashed tag bits is H = TAGW − (LOGLB − 2), and MPKI is a step function of it: designs with H ≥ 6 average 5.4 MPKI, designs with H ≤ 5 average 9.5, and nothing lands between. Tag aliasing is a cliff, not a slope.

B. Finding 2: the gem5 tier does not discriminate

Across the ten designs promoted to Tier 2, gem5 core IPC at the shipped depth spans 0.8867–0.8912: a total spread of 0.51%. Tier 2 as configured cannot separate these designs, and this must be stated before any correlation computed on it is believed.

It is worth showing why. Regressing Tier-1 MPKI against gem5 depth-9 IPC over all ten designs gives r = −0.908, which reads as strong cross-simulator agreement. Removing the single outlier gen005_0 (Tier-1 MPKI = 16.15, the only design far from the pack) collapses it to −0.420. Removing one further point, gen008_1, inverts it to +0.884. A correlation that changes sign on the removal of two of ten points is not evidence of anything. We report it because the per-generation τ(VFS, IPC) = +1.00 printed by our own harness is likewise degenerate—it is computed over the single design promoted per generation—and should not be quoted.

C. Finding 3: depth robustness is governed by accuracy

We rank the seven archived elites by VFS at each depth and compare against the shipped depth of nine by Kendall's τ over all 21 pairs (Table III). The ranking is exactly stable to depth twelve and then degrades.

The decay is not diffuse. Every discordant pair, at every depth, has gen006_2 on one side. Remove that one design and τ = +1.000 at all eight depths. And gen006_2 is precisely the archived elite with high MPKI: 9.220 against 5.08–5.46 for the other six. Its tag width was cut to TAGW = 8, giving H = 4 hashed tag bits and eight times the tag aliasing of the seed, while its first-level gshare was doubled—accuracy traded for bandwidth.

By Equation (5), C scales with mpi · D. A design with 1.7× the misprediction rate loses ground 1.7× faster as D grows, so it must eventually cross the others; depth 16 is where it does. Depth robustness is thus a direct function of accuracy—and accuracy is exactly the quantity Finding 1 shows the depth-nine score does not measure. The score is blind to the property that determines whether its own ranking survives.

D. The archive

Seven of 144 nominal cells are occupied, which reads as 4.9% coverage and invites the conclusion that the search barely explored. That conclusion is wrong, and the correct one is more interesting. Across all 29 designs, only two EPI bands, two p1 values and two p2 values were ever realized: the reachable sub-grid is 2×2×2 = 8 cells, and the search occupies seven of them. The grid is mis-specified for this design space—four of six EPI bands lie below any achievable predictor energy—rather than under-explored. Notably, gen006_2 scores worse than the seed (0.7855 vs. 0.8784) and survives only because it occupies a different cell. The design that breaks the depth ranking entered the archive through the diversity mechanism, not through the score.

VIII. Threats to Validity

In-sample scores. Every VFS here is measured on the 126 traces the search optimized against. The 42 held-out traces remain unscored.

One seed, one operator. The offline arm resizes tables; it cannot invent an algorithm. Findings 1 and 3 concern the metric's response to a population, and that population is a parameter family. A structurally different predictor could move p2 or EPI into regions we never reached.

Tier-2 configuration, not gem5. Finding 2 is a statement about our gem5 configuration—5×106 instructions, one promoted design per generation, SE-mode workloads—not about gem5's capability. A longer window or a wider promotion budget could well restore discrimination.

Small elite set. Table III rests on seven elites and 21 pairs. The attribution to gen006_2 is unambiguous within this set, but the set is small.

IX. Conclusions

VFS is a considerable improvement on ranking predictors by misprediction rate, but as an optimization target it behaves as a throughput metric with minor corrections. An automated search given VFS alone finds width, not accuracy: the best design we found is the seed with a wider fetch line and slightly worse MPKI. Because CBP-NG assumes a fixed nine-stage penalty, and because misprediction cost is the only depth-dependent term, the score is structurally blind to the one property—accuracy—that decides whether its ranking survives a deeper machine. A single high-MPKI design accounts for every rank inversion we observed. We suggest that cost-aware predictor metrics be reported at more than one depth, which the re-scoring identity of Equation (5) makes nearly free, and that automated searches over such metrics report which term is actually carrying the optimizer.
